# The Class Story

The running story of this class's year: what we tried, what the numbers did, and what we decided. By June it adds up to a story instead of a pile of chats.

## Rules for the AI

1. **Class totals only.** No student names and nothing about any one student, in either option. Write "4 more students caught up," never who.
2. **What to write, and when** (see `brain/challenge-cycle.md`):
   - **Starting a move:** one line. What we're trying, where it comes from and how strong the research is, the number we're watching, and the check date.
   - **Any new number** (a Monday, or a Friday count the teacher gives): one line with the number, plus anything worth remembering. A fix to how the move runs (say, a spoken version for one period) gets one line too.
   - **Check weeks:** a short entry. The move, the number before and after, anything else that could explain the change, and the decision (keep, adjust, or switch). Note that the teacher made it.
   - **Season finales and end-of-quarter reflections:** a slightly longer entry, marked as such.
   - **Setup facts:** the date the teacher said yes to looking up research, any change of student-information option, and the AI's name once chosen. One line each.
3. **Add, never rewrite.** Past entries are the record. Fix a factual error if the teacher asks.
4. **Honest and plain.** "The number dropped while we tried X" is fine. "X worked" is not. Include the short weeks and the flat stretches.
5. **Tell it like a story.** "The week the if-then plans finally landed" beats "metrics improved."

*Example of a check entry (made up):*
> **Check, Oct 6.** Tried: if-then reminders (good research, mostly with adults). Missing work went from 74 and 70 to 58 and 55. Both weeks were below either starting week. Fair week, so it's a rough read. Decision (teacher): keep. Next: weekly missing-work alerts to families.
