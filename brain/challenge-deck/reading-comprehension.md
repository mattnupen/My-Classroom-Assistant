# Challenge: Past the Words
*"We're working on the part after you can read the sentence: what it actually means, and how you'd prove it."*

**The problem.** Students who can decode every word still finish the page holding nothing. Nothing flags them as struggling readers, and "read it again" is the only advice on offer. What's missing is a few habits nobody ever taught them out loud.

**Ask first:** "When they read in pairs or groups now, what does it look like?"

**Get to know it** (first chat, multiple choice; see `../first-chat.md`):
- *Card 1*
  1. **Group reading** (the "Ask first") — "When they read in pairs or groups now, what does it look like?" · *Mostly off task* · *They read but don't talk about it* · *A few groups really discuss it* · *We don't read in groups yet*
  2. **Struggle** (multi-select) — "What do they struggle with most?" · *Remembering what they read* · *Big ideas vs. details* · *Hard words* · *Finding evidence in the text*
  3. **Texts** — "What do they mostly read?" · *Novels or chapter books* · *Textbook chapters* · *Articles* · *A mix*
- *Card 2*
  1. **Range** — "Do some kids read well below grade level?" · *Yes, several* · *A few* · *Not that I know of* · *Not sure*

What the answers point to: read but don't talk → the strategy card gives pairs something to say; several well below grade level → plan a way in for them (read aloud first, a shorter passage) and suggest checking reading support with the case manager; use their actual texts in everything you make.

**The number to watch. In one reading lesson a week, during the pair or group part, count how many pairs or groups **use a strategy without being prompted** (predict, question, clarify, summarize, or point to a line as evidence). Report it as, for example, "5 of 8 groups." No extra class time, and no names.

**Why not a reading test?** Be straight with the teacher about this. Comprehension grows slowly. The common quick weekly reading checks grow slowly, passages vary in difficulty from week to week, and scores rise with normal growth anyway. So over a few weeks a weekly score would mostly show noise. What the research says drives the gains is students using the strategies on their own, and that *can* change within weeks. This count is a practical measure, not a tested one. If the teacher wants a test number too, offer a three-minute reading check at the start and end of the year (the pack explains how). Don't give it every week.

## Week 1: start now

The teacher's answer to "Ask first" is the starting number (it's often zero, and that's fine). If a pair or group reading task happens before the first strategy lesson, count it quietly, but don't wait for more. Start move 1 this week; the first check will be a rough read. Tell the class these same few strategies are all they'll get, and that by the end they'll use them without the card.

## The moves, one at a time (about two weeks each)

1. **Teach three or four strategies, out loud.** Model predict, clarify, and summarize on a real text, and reuse the same prompt card every week. *Strong research behind this (EEF Toolkit; WWC adolescent literacy guide, 2008).*
2. **Reciprocal teaching roles.** Four role cards. You model each role, then fade out. *Good research, with limits (Rosenshine & Meister, 1994): the gains are real, but much smaller on standardized tests.*
3. **Discuss the text, with evidence.** Questions with more than one defensible answer: think, pair, share, and "find the line that proves it." *Good research, with limits: the direct evidence is moderate (WWC, grades 4–12); the broader research on talk is strong.*
4. **Before a hard text: key words and background.** Three or four words and one paragraph on what the text assumes you know. This supports whichever move is running. *Strong research behind teaching vocabulary (WWC, grades 4–12).*

## Watch out

- **No popcorn reading and no minutes-only reading logs.** Little benefit, and most of the room tunes out.
- **Don't collect the strategy cards.** Once the cards become a worksheet to hand in, the strategies stop being a reading habit.
- **Expect slow movement.** "We kept doing it and the groups got more independent" is a good season, even if nothing else moved yet.
